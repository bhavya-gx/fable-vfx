# How it works

A tour of the pieces, in the order the pipeline runs them. Each one is a short Python file in `vfx/`.

## 1. Prep (`render.py prep`, `vfx/grade.py`)

ffmpeg trims the raw clip, scales it to the output size, and applies a colour grade as a 3D LUT. The LUT is generated by `grade.py` from three numbers so it can be reproduced exactly in numpy and in ffmpeg. The look is "clean room": phone footage is usually lit by a window in front (skin looks right) and a tungsten lamp behind (walls go mustard), so the grade pulls saturation only from a narrow yellow hue band, nudges those pixels a touch brighter, lifts the mids slightly and keeps black and white pinned. Skin sits about 30 degrees away from that hue band and is untouched.

Audio is pulled to `voice.wav` at the same trim, so picture and voice line up by construction.

## 2. Matte and hands (`tools/matte.swift`)

A 150 line Swift program reads `base.mp4` with AVFoundation and runs two Apple Vision requests on every frame: person segmentation (quality `.accurate`) and hand pose (21 joints per hand). It writes one 8 bit greyscale PNG per frame and a JSON line per frame with the joints in normalised coordinates. On device, no model download, about 20 frames a second on an M-series chip.

Vision gives one extra leading frame compared to ffmpeg's decode, so `matte.offset` is 1 by default. If you bring your own mattes, set it to match.

## 3. Clean plate (`vfx/media.py: build_plate`)

The Vision matte is good but soft at the edges. To get hair and finger edges right during the background swap we need to know what the room looks like *behind* the person. The plate is the mean of every pixel over all frames where the (dilated) matte says the person is not there. The region the person never leaves (behind the torso) is filled by pushing known colours inward at increasing blur radii, so a lookup never hits black.

## 4. Edge refinement (`vfx/media.py: refine`)

In a band around the silhouette, each pixel's alpha is estimated from where its colour sits on the line between the room colour behind it (from the plate, exposure matched to the current frame) and the person's colour next to it (their own pixels blurred outward). Where the two colours are too close to tell apart, it falls back to the Vision matte. The result is clamped so clearly-inside pixels stay solid and far-outside pixels stay empty.

The swap itself is `I + (1 - alpha) * (new - B)`: instead of blending the new background over a semi-transparent edge pixel (which keeps a fringe of the old wall), it subtracts the old room colour and adds the new one. A soft "light wrap" of the blurred ocean is added at the edges so the person looks lit by the new scene, plus a small daylight tint.

## 5. The ocean (`vfx/ocean.py`)

Entirely procedural, rendered per frame in numpy:

- A flat sea seen from a camera three metres up with a 1300 px focal length. Each screen row below the horizon maps to a distance on the sea.
- Waves are 13 sine components with wavelengths from 0.7 m up, random directions, deep-water dispersion (`w = sqrt(g k)`), amplitude attenuated by how many pixels a wavelength covers so distant waves do not alias.
- Shading is a Fresnel mix of a deep water colour and the reflected sky, plus a sun glint (`rs^380 * 7 + rs^28 * 0.3`), plus distance haze toward the horizon colour.
- The sky is a vertical gradient with a sun glow and drifting clouds made from five octaves of upscaled noise, thresholded and tinted.
- A final blur and a bloom on the highlights sell the glow.

The ring wipe in and out is a distance field from the hand position (either detected or configured), eased outward over the wipe time, with a bright rim and a flash at the origin.

## 6. Props (`vfx/sprites.py`)

A flat mask (a star polygon drawn with PIL, or a letter set in the bundled font) becomes a 3D-looking sprite:

- A distance transform inside the mask gives a height field shaped like a rounded cushion.
- Sobel gradients of the height give normals; one directional light produces diffuse and specular, plus a rim light from above.
- The colour is a top-to-bottom gradient of the palette's `top` and `bottom`.
- The `side` colour is stamped 16 times at a diagonal offset, back to front, to extrude it.

On screen (`vfx/scene.py`) a prop is anchored to the palm centre (mean of wrist and the four knuckles), with the hand track interpolated across frames where detection dropped and smoothed with a Gaussian kernel. Entrances: `fly` arcs in from a point with a comet tail of glow blobs and spins into place; `pop` rises out of the palm. After landing: a damped bounce in scale, a slow sway and bob, a coloured glow, a burst of sparkles, then idle twinkles. The vanish is a quick scale-up-and-shrink with a white flash and one last burst.

## 7. Title (`vfx/scene.py`)

The title is rendered once as a mask, sized to a fraction of the frame width. On each frame it is drawn with `(1 - matte)` so the person covers it, with a soft shadow for separation, fading in over six frames and out over `title.fade`.

## 8. Sound (`vfx/sfx.py`)

Everything is synthesised at 48 kHz:

- **Whoosh**: band-passed white noise under a sine squared envelope, a brighter band at the peak.
- **Sea**: low-passed noise slowly amplitude modulated, plus a quieter high band for spray, faded in after the first snap and out at the second.
- **Chime**: a run of sine notes (C7 to E8) with a second partial, short attack, exponential decay. Ascending when a prop lands, descending at the vanish.
- **Pop**: a sine sweep from 700 Hz down with a fast decay.

Timings are taken from the config, so the sounds never drift from the picture.

## 9. Mux (`render.py mux`)

ffmpeg cleans the voice (high-pass, denoise, loudness normalise), mixes in the effects, applies the speed change with `setpts` and `atempo`, and encodes the final with `faststart` so it streams.

## Why this design

Every effect reads a few numbers from the config and nothing else, so adding a new one means a new module and a new config key, and old configs keep rendering exactly as before. The first version had all of these numbers as constants at the top of one file. Moving them out is the whole difference between "a video I made" and "a tool you can use".
